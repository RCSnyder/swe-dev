# Theoretical Minimum Dossier Contract

The dossier is one self-contained Markdown file. It must remain auditable after the chat session and client-specific citation UI disappear.

## Frontmatter

Use YAML frontmatter with at least:

```yaml
---
generator: swe-dev-theoretical-minimum-finder
artifact_version: 1
topic: "<topic>"
target_capability: "<observable serious-work capability>"
intended_learner: "<learner profile>"
entry_assumptions: "<knowledge and tools assumed before this minimum>"
generated_at: "<ISO date/time if available>"
status: grounded | mixed | limited | exploratory
scope: "<one-line scope>"
source_counts:
  tier_0: 0
  tier_1: 0
  tier_2: 0
  tier_3: 0
  tier_4: 0
  tier_5: 0
limitations:
  - "<important limitation, or []>"
---
```

Status guidance:

- `grounded` — core rests on inspected Tier 0–2 material and material disputes/gaps are represented.
- `mixed` — most core is grounded, but meaningful sections remain tentative/inferred.
- `limited` — browsing/source access/tooling prevented adequate grounding.
- `exploratory` — the user explicitly asked for frontier/speculative synthesis or evidence is inherently immature.

Do not use `grounded` merely because the file is polished.

## Inline provenance

Use stable source IDs inside prose:

```markdown
The happened-before relation is a partial order over events generated by local order,
message send/receive order, and transitivity. [S07]
```

If a paragraph's central statement is agent-generated rather than directly sourced, begin it with one of:

```markdown
[SYNTHESIS]
[INFERENCE]
[UNKNOWN]
[CONTESTED]
```

A synthesis paragraph may still cite sources that motivated it.

Do not rely on client-rendered citation tokens, footnote state, or chat history as the only provenance. The file must resolve its own `[Sxx]` identifiers.

## Minimum-unit schema

Give each retained unit a stable `Mxx` identifier:

```markdown
### [M01] <unit name>
- Own without notes: <argument, model, method, or evidence chain to regenerate>
- Enables: <target performances and later units>
- Depends on: <other Mxx units or declared entry assumptions>
- Removal failure: <specific capability that breaks if M01 is removed>
- Non-redundancy: <why retained units cannot cheaply replace M01>
- Regeneration test: <closed-book task>
- Defense obligation: <question or challenge>
- Sources: [S01] [S04]
```

Each unit needs at least one content-verified source or an explicit `[SYNTHESIS]` qualification explaining that the organization is proposed rather than field-authoritative. Dependency and necessity claims may be syntheses even when the unit's content is grounded; mark them honestly.

Do not force a unit count. Prefer the smallest set that remains closed over prerequisites and adequate for the target performances.

## Dependency-map schema

Show how target performances depend on minimum units and how units depend on one another. Use a small table, adjacency list, or Mermaid graph. Keep declared entry assumptions outside the minimum but connect them explicitly.

Flag:

- unsupported dependency edges;
- cycles that may require co-teaching;
- hidden prerequisites;
- useful adjacent material that is outside the target scope.

## Exclusion schema

Compression must be inspectable:

| Candidate | Decision | Why excluded or deferred | Revisit when | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ... | excluded \| deferred | ... | ... | [Sxx] or [SYNTHESIS] |

An exclusion means the target remains achievable without the candidate. A deferral means the candidate becomes necessary only beyond the pinned scope or after the initial minimum.

## Deep-structure schema

For each important structure include some subset of:

```markdown
### <name>
- Structure / invariant:
- What it constrains:
- What follows from it:
- Boundary / counterexample:
- Transfer to adjacent domains:
- Sources: [Sxx]
```

Favor invariants, impossibility results, state models, conservation/accounting identities, causal mechanisms, and evidentiary standards over fashionable vocabulary.

## Minimum-source-spine schema

For each source include enough to audit selection:

| Work | Supports units | Role | Why it survives deletion | Tier | Verification | Access | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|

The `Sources` column may cite curricula or reviews that justify pedagogical status; the work's own registry entry proves identity/content, not that the learner needs it.

Only include free/open links that were actually confirmed. Distinguish a publisher/official page from a full-text link.

Do not pad the spine to a round number. A source survives only when it grounds a minimum unit, supplies a high-value derivation or problem set, or covers several retained units better than available alternatives.

## Mastery examination standard

The examination should reveal whether the learner can regenerate, use, and defend the minimum. State allowed resources and intervention rules before the tasks.

Default:

- at least one blank-page regeneration obligation for each unit, combining units where useful;
- 3 unseen or transfer problems;
- 2 oral defenses around source arguments, solutions, or competing models;
- 1 integrated challenge spanning multiple units;
- 1 shared process rubric.

Strong prompt forms:

- derive the relation/result from assumptions;
- construct the smallest counterexample;
- diagnose which assumption failed;
- compare two models under a changed boundary condition;
- rebuild an argument without the source;
- defend a source as if you wrote it, including its limitations;
- transfer the invariant to a novel case and state where the analogy breaks.

The process rubric must name evidence for:

- framing and assumptions;
- representation choice;
- decomposition and intermediate derivation;
- checks against invariants, evidence, units, or counterexamples;
- recovery from a dead end;
- boundary conditions and uncertainty;
- clarity under questioning.

Do not award mastery for an unsupported correct answer. Do not penalize an unconventional route merely because it differs from an expected solution.

For training, require a real attempt before revealing help. Use the hint ladder in order: orienting question, relevant principle, partial structure, then a worked solution only when requested or when the declared training protocol allows it. Time-box productive struggle and record which hint level was needed.

If reference solutions are requested, keep them compact and emphasize reasoning obligations, assumptions, acceptable alternate routes, and decisive checks.

## Contradiction register

Use a table:

| Issue | Position A | Evidence A | Position B | Evidence B | Current assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not create a row merely to appear balanced. Include only material disagreements or unresolved definitions.

## Claims and evidence ledger

Capture the most load-bearing claims, not every sentence:

| Claim ID | Claim | Status | Evidence | What would falsify / weaken it | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | ... | grounded | [S03] [S04] | ... | high |

Allowed status values:

`grounded`, `cross-checked`, `contested`, `tentative`, `inference`, `synthesis`, `unknown`.

Separate confidence from consequence. A low-probability claim can be high-consequence; do not collapse the two.

## Source Registry schema

Every cited source gets exactly one registry entry:

```markdown
### [S01] <Title>
- Author / institution: <...>
- Date / edition: <...>
- Tier: 1
- Type: paper | book | standard | dataset | official-doc | code | review | teaching | secondary | user-source
- URL / identifier: <direct URL or DOI/identifier>
- Accessed: <date>
- Verification: content | existence-only | blocked | unverified
- Supports: <specific claims/sections>
- Limitations: <scope, model, age, conflict, access limitation>
```

Rules:

- No registry entry without a real identifier or explicit `URL / identifier: unavailable` for a user-provided/local source.
- `content` means relevant content was inspected.
- `existence-only` sources must not support substantive factual claims.
- Search snippets do not count as content verification.
- A DOI or URL should not be invented from citation-like memory.

## Audit result

End with a compact self-audit:

```markdown
## Audit result
- Provenance integrity: pass | qualified | fail
- Target capability clarity: <assessment>
- Prerequisite closure: <assessment>
- Deletion-test strength: <assessment>
- Core Tier 1-2 coverage: <assessment>
- Counterevidence search: <assessment>
- Mastery-exam validity: <assessment>
- Link/content verification: <assessment>
- Known hallucination risks removed: <list>
- Remaining uncertainty: <list>
- Structural lint: pass | warnings | not run
```

If provenance, prerequisite closure, or a load-bearing deletion case fails, the artifact status cannot be `grounded`.

## Single-artifact rule

The Source Registry, source spine, examination, uncertainty ledger, and audit must all live in this same file. Do not externalize them into supporting project artifacts.
